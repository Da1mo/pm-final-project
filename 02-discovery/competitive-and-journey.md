# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A long-time, heavily engaged subscriber who opens the app most evenings out of habit.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To find something good to watch tonight quickly, without the decision feeling like work.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Priya scrolled for twenty minutes through 15,000 titles, closed the app without watching anything, and put on a DVD. Aisha has stopped trying to discover anything and re-watches the same four comfort shows. Half the focus group said the amount of choice made them anxious.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** The only outside tool the research shows for this persona is Priya’s DVD collection. The research doesn’t show this persona using any digital tools such as review sites, list apps or other streaming services. YouTube does come up, but for Raj, who left because of a playback bug, not because he couldn’t choose.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Open the app in the evening, out of habit.
Scroll the home rows and catalogue for about twenty minutes, looking for something that fits the mood.
Fail to decide, and close the app without watching anything.
Go to the DVD shelf and pick something familiar from a small set.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment it breaks is the end of step 2. After twenty minutes of effort they still have nothing they want to watch and give up. The size of the catalogue is supposed to be the app’s strength, but at that point it’s what drives them away.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Priya (UXR-01): “There’s 15,000 titles but nothing I actually want. I ended up going back to a DVD.”
Aisha (UXR-03): “Discovering anything new feels like a chore, so I don’t.”
Focus group (UXR-12): three of six participants said the sheer number of choices made them anxious and they’d rather someone “just tell me what’s good tonight.”
